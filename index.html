For millennia, humanity has sought luxury in the material — gold, space, exclusivity. But the ultimate luxury, the one we never thought would exist, is the ability to truly step outside of ourselves. To look into the eyes of another species, to communicate, and to feel felt in return. We are not just building software; we are dismantling the boundary between ‘human’ and ‘nature.’ This is the age of interspecies empathy.

Every song is data
before it is music.

every species lives inside its own sensory world.

Biologists call it the umwelt. Ours and a bird’s barely overlap, and almost everything we say falls outside the part we share.

What exists today

  • apps that chirp back.
  • collars that claim to translate.
  • recorded calls on a loop.

Novelty treats a living signal as a sound effect. The bird hears noise, and the person hears a joke.

understanding is a
modeling problem.

A birdsong is not a word. It is pitch, timing and amplitude shaped at millisecond scale, read by a listener whose hearing is not ours. Reaching it takes signal physics, perceptual models and years of field data, not a soundboard.

Research timeline2009 — 2026

seventeen years
of listening.

2009

  1. M.01 · Founded

    a lab that listens first.

    Morsis begins as a three-person bioacoustics group with one rule: no device ever touches an animal. Software and air only.

    3 researchers · 0 species mapped

  2. M.02 · Field archive

    forty thousand hours of song.

    A distributed recording network starts building the annotated archive every later model is trained on.

    40,000 hours annotated

  3. M.03 · First complete map

    one species, 100% mapped.

    Every syllable, call and regional dialect of the zebra finch, Taeniopygia guttata, charted end to end: the first full vocal map of any species.

    100% repertoire · 1 species

  4. M.04 · Intent decoded

    alarm is not contact.

    The first harmonic-intent model separates alarm, contact and courtship calls across twelve songbird species without being told which is which.

    91% intent accuracy · 12 species

  5. M.05 · Scale

    a thousand voices.

    Transfer learning across related species turns months of mapping into days. The atlas passes one thousand species.

    1,000 species mapped

  6. M.06 · Beta model

    the first beta model.

    Model β.1 opens to eleven partner ornithology labs: phrase in, avian-band signal out, with every transmission logged for review.

    11 partner labs · β.1

  7. M.07 · First answer

    a wren answers back.

    In Chamber 004, a wild Eurasian wren replies to a synthesized contact call with its own, under the non-invasive protocol. Replicated 214 times.

    214 verified exchanges

  8. M.08 · Half the sky

    half of all birds, mapped.

    The atlas crosses five thousand species, and the protocol draft opens for public peer review.

    5,500 species · 50%

  9. M.09 · Public release

    naiva.

    Naiva, the first publicly accessible Morsis model. Anyone can compose a phrase and hear it shaped for a bird’s hearing.

    6,831 species · 62% mapped

We build to understand,never to command.

Morsis is a software research lab. We make no collars, no gadgets and no novelty apps: only signals, models, and the ethics to use them well.

  1. [V.01]

    empathy over mastery.

    Technology designed not to domesticate or command, but to establish mutual, respectful understanding between species.

  2. [V.02]

    rigor and wonder.

    We treat biological mystery with mathematical precision, and with reverence. Every claim is modeled, measured and open to review.

  3. [V.03]

    the ultimate luxury.

    True connection beyond human boundaries: a paradigm shift in how we perceive intelligence, and life.
